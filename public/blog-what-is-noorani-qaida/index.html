What Is Noorani Qaida? A Complete Beginner’s Guide

Noorani Qaida is a beginner’s book that teaches children and adults to read the Quran from zero. It starts with the single Arabic letters and moves step by step through joined letters, vowels, Tanween, Madd, Sukoon and Tashdeed, until the student can read words straight from the Quran. It is named after its compiler, Shaykh Noor Muhammad Ludhianvi, and it is the first Quran book for millions of children in Pakistan, India, the United Kingdom and beyond.

You can open the whole Qaida free in our online Noorani Qaida, with a short test after every lesson and a free PDF.

Why Noorani Qaida is important

Reading the Quran correctly depends on two things: recognising every letter and saying it from its right place. The Qaida builds both, one small step at a time. A child who finishes it properly can read new Quran words alone, with the basic rules of Tajweed already in place. A child who rushes it carries the same mistakes into the Quran for years.

Who should learn Noorani Qaida?

  • Children starting the Quran, usually from about 4 or 5 years old.
  • Adults who never learnt to read Arabic, or who want to fix weak foundations. Our Quran for Adults course starts here too.
  • Reverts learning the Arabic script for the first time.
  • Anyone who reads the Quran but stumbles over letters or vowels.

How many pages are in Noorani Qaida?

Most printed editions have around 30 to 40 small pages, depending on the publisher and the size of the print. Our online version has 31 pages. The number of pages matters less than finishing each lesson properly.

How long does it take to finish Noorani Qaida?

These times assume a 30-minute class five days a week. At that pace, most children finish in about four to eight months, and adults often finish in two to four months. With fewer classes a week, it takes a few months longer. The first lessons take the longest, because every later lesson depends on them. Moving on only when a lesson is fully correct is faster in the end.

How to read Noorani Qaida: tips for parents

  1. Practise for 10 to 15 minutes every day rather than once a week.
  2. Read from right to left, and point to each letter as your child reads.
  3. Mix the order so your child recognises letters and does not just memorise rows.
  4. Ask about dots and vowels: “Which letter is this? Which vowel is on it?”
  5. Use the test at the end of each lesson in our online Qaida before moving on.

Common mistakes students make

  • Saying the heavy letters خ ص ض غ ط ق ظ (Khaa, Suaad, Duaad, Ghain, Tuaa, Qaaf, Zuaa) as light letters.
  • Mixing up ح (Haa, from the middle of the throat) and ه (Ha, from the deepest part of the throat), or ع (Ain) and ء (Hamzah).
  • Stretching short vowels, or cutting long ones short. A short vowel is one count; a long vowel is two.
  • Reading Sukoon letters as if they had a vowel.
  • Rushing ahead to the Quran before the Qaida is strong.

What comes after Noorani Qaida?

After the Qaida, most students begin reading the Quran itself, often starting with Juz Amma because its surahs are short and familiar. Juz Amma is the 30th and last of the Quran’s 30 parts (a juz, or para in Urdu), from Surah An-Naba to Surah An-Nas. From there they move to full Quran Reading, then deeper Tajweed, and for some, Hifz, which means memorising the whole Quran by heart. Our free Tajweed Rules page is a good next step at home.

Noorani Qaida for adults and reverts

Adults often worry that the Qaida is “for children”. It is simply the clearest way to learn to read the Quran, at any age. Adults usually move faster because they understand rules and can practise alone. Reverts who are new to the Arabic script find that the Qaida’s step-by-step order removes the fear of a page full of unfamiliar letters.

Printed Qaida or online Qaida?

Both teach the same lessons. A printed book is easy to carry and has no screen. An online Qaida can add tests, larger letters and colour, and it works on any phone. Many families use both: the online version for practice and tests, and a printed copy or our free PDF for reading with the teacher.

Indo-Pak script or Uthmani script?

Noorani Qaida is printed in the Indo-Pak script, the style of most Qurans printed in Pakistan, India, Bangladesh and the United Kingdom. Qurans printed in Saudi Arabia and the Arab world usually use the Uthmani (Madinah) script. The letters, words and rules are the same; only some signs look different, such as Sukoon, the standing vowels and the marks for long vowels. Our online Qaida and PDF use the Indo-Pak script. If your child will read from a Madinah Mushaf, tell the teacher, who can show the differences in a lesson or two.

Why learn Noorani Qaida with a teacher?

A page can show the letters, but only a teacher can hear whether your child says ع (Ain), ق (Qaaf) or ض (Duaad) correctly and fix it before it becomes a habit.
