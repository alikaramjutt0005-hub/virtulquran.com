Chapter 2Takhti 2Murakkabat
(1) Help the child recognise joined letters one by one. For example, in لا the letter on the right is Laam and the letter on the left is Alif. (2) Teach letters of the same shape by changing their dots around. For example, if the dot of ب is placed above it, which letter does it become? And if the dots of ت are placed below it, which letter will it be? What is the difference between ت and ق, and between ش and ث? Note For the child's ease and the beauty of the Qaida, every letter in the whole Qaida has been given a separate colour, except the bold letters.

Chapter 4Takhti 4Harakat
Instructions Teach the harakat like this: Zabar is above ـَ, Zer is below ـِ, and Pesh is above and curved ـُ. Put the harakat on letters and show the child. Do not let the harakat be stretched at all. Also do not let the child read so fast that a jerk is felt, and do not let the sound become unclear (majhool). Note An Alif that has a Jazm or any harakat on it is called Hamza. In the lesson of harakat the order of the letters' makharij has been kept. So tell the child that these two letters ء ه, or these three letters ط د ت, are read from the same place and are recognised separately because of their sifaat.

Chapter 5Takhti 5Tanween
Instructions Two Zabar ـً, two Zer ـٍ and two Pesh ـٌ are called Tanween. Use this lesson of Tanween to practise Ghunnah. Ghunnah means hiding the sound in the nose. To make it easy for the child, explain the rule of Ghunnah on Tanween like this: if any of the letters ء ه ع ح غ خ ل ر comes after Tanween, there is no Ghunnah; if any other letter comes, there is Ghunnah. Note Ask the child why there is Ghunnah here and why there is none there, and listen to the whole rule in the answer. Note In the Tanween of Zabar the Alif is not named, and the same goes for Ya.

Instructions Here have the child spell the words out, and show the child yourself how the letters join. If the children cannot say the letters or the harakat correctly, repeat what was read before. Until the children start spelling out by themselves, never give the next lesson. Never give up this principle, otherwise your effort and the child's years will be wasted. Chapter 7Takhti 7Khari Zabar, Khari Zer, Ulta Pesh
Instructions Khari Zabar, Khari Zer and Ulta Pesh are read stretched to the length of one Alif. Khari Zabar stands in place of Alif Madda, Khari Zer in place of Ya Madda, and Ulta Pesh in place of Waw Madda. Note Compare with Takhti 4 of harakat, and explain well the difference between the lying and the standing harakat. Note Keep the sound of the letters of Madd that come after Noon and Meem from going into the nose. For example, in نَا نُوْا نِیْ مَا مُوْا مِیْ do not let the sound of Alif, Waw and Ya go into the nose; have it said from the empty part of the mouth.

Chapter 8Takhti 8Madd wa Leen
Instructions The letters of Madd are ا و ی. These three are called letters of Madd when there is a Zabar before Alif (Alif Madda), a Pesh before Waw Saakin (Waw Madda), and a Zer before Ya Saakin (Ya Madda). These letters are read stretched to the length of one Alif. This is called Madd Asli, as in بَا بُوْا بِیْ. And if after these letters of Madd there is a cause of Madd, a Hamza or a Sukoon (original, or one caused by stopping), the Madd is long and is called Madd Far'i. Note Another name for Jazm is Sukoon. The letter that has a Sukoon on it is called Saakin.

Chapter 9Takhti 9Mashq HarakatKhari Zabar, Khari Zer, Ulta Pesh, Madd wa Leen wa Tanween
Rule 1 If a Hamza ء comes after the letters of Madd, the Madd is always four Alifs long. If the Hamza is in the same word, it is called Madd Muttasil and Madd Wajib, as in جَآءَ. If the letters of Madd are at the end of the first word and the Hamza is at the start of the next word, it is called Madd Munfasil and Madd Jaiz, as in اِنَّاۤ اَنْزَلْنَا. Rule 2 If a Sukoon comes after the letters of Madd or the letters of Leen, there is Madd there too. If the Sukoon was already there, it is called Madd Lazim and its length is five Alifs, as in آٰلْـٰٔنَ. If the Sukoon is because of stopping (Waqf), it is called Madd Waqfi and Madd Aarzi, and its length is three Alifs, as when you stop on جُوْعٍ ۟ خَوْفٍ ۟ and the like. Rule 3 If ر has a Zabar or Pesh on it, it is read full, and if it has a Zer, ر is read thin. Note Practise Ghunnah here too in the earlier way: if ء ه ع ح غ خ ل ر comes after Tanween there is no Ghunnah; if any letter other than these eight comes there is Ghunnah. Ask the child the reason for Ghunnah and for no Ghunnah. Also teach the child to stop (Waqf) at the end of every word. When stopping on a sentence, if the last letter is not Saakin, make it Saakin and end both the voice and the breath. When stopping on the round ة it is read as ه. Note Both of these ways of spelling out a Madd are correct: Jeem Alif Madd Zabar جَآ, Hamza Zabar ءَ, جَآءَ; or Jeem Zabar Alif Madd جَآ, Hamza Zabar ءَ, جَآءَ. In the same way Jeem Khari Zer Madd جِآ, Hamza Zabar یْءَ, جِایْٓءَ.

Chapter 10Takhti 10Sukoon yani Jazm
Teach the shape and the name of the Jazm, and have the answers to these questions memorised as well. Question What does a Jazm look like? Answer Curved. Question What is a letter with a Jazm called? Answer Saakin. Question How many times is a letter with a Jazm read? Answer Only once, joined with the letter before it. In this lesson take special care that the letters are said correctly, and explain well the difference between letters that sound alike. For example ث س ص, and ذ ز ظ, and in the same way ت ط and ق ك must be told apart. A child who is not able is excused, but the effort should go on.

Chapter 11Takhti 11Mashq Sukoon
Question When is there Ghunnah on Noon Saakin and Tanween? Answer When no throat letter ء ه ع ح غ خ and no ل ر comes after them. Question When is Ra Saakin رْ read full? Answer When there is no Ya Saakin before it, and no original Zer before it in the same word. And if a letter of Isti'la comes after Ra Saakin in the same word, it is full in every case; if it comes before, there is a choice. The letters of Isti'la, that is the full letters, are these: ص ض ط ظ خ غ ق. Signs of Waqf ○ Waqf Taam. م Waqf Lazim. ط Waqf Mutlaq. ج Waqf Jaiz. س Saktah: the breath should not break. وقفه a slightly longer Saktah. ز Waqf Mujawwaz. لا If you are forced to stop on it, go back and read it joined; do not begin from after لا. Note Teach the child to stop at the end of every word.

Answer Mushaddad. Question How many times is a letter with a Tashdeed read? Answer Twice: once joined with the letter before it, and the second time by itself. Question How is a Mushaddad letter read? Answer Firmly, with a slight pause.

Chapter 16Takhti 16Tashdeed baad Huroof-e-Madda
Have the child spell it out like this: Saad Alif Laam Zabar ضَآلَّ, Laam two Zabar لًا, ضَآلًّا. Waw Zabar وَ, Saad Fa Khari Zabar صّٰٓفْ, Fa Khari Zabar فّٰ, وَالصّٰٓفّٰ, Ta Zer تِ, وَالصّٰٓفّٰتِ.

الْكُبْرٰی ؕ
Khatima: Ijra-e-Qawaid
(1) If, after Noon Saakin or Tanween, the next word begins with one of the letters of Yarmaloon ی ر م ل و ن, there is Idghaam: without Ghunnah in Laam and Ra, and with Ghunnah in the other four letters. Explain to the children that the letter with a Jazm before a Tashdeed is usually not read, as in مِنْ رَّبِّكَ. (2) If a Hamza-tul-Wasl comes after Tanween, a Noon with a Zer under it is put there and joined to the next letter, and in place of the Tanween only one harakat is left. This is called Noon Qutni.
30
Note The Alif that is written in the Tanween of Zabar is not read. (3) If ب comes after Noon Saakin or Tanween, both of them turn into Meem, and there is Ghunnah on them as well. (4) If ب or م comes after Meem Saakin, there is Ghunnah in the Meem Saakin. (5) If there is a Zer before the Laam of the word Allah, the Laam is read thin, as in بِسْمِ اللّٰهِ. And if there is a Zabar or Pesh before the Laam, the Laam is read full, as in اَللّٰهُ ، نَارُ اللّٰهِ.
