Level 1 · EasyAllah, Our Creator

Who Allah is and what a Muslim believes about Him.

Who is Allah?

  • Allah is the One who created us, the earth, the sky and everything in them.
  • Allah is One. He has no partner, no parents and no children.
  • Nobody made Allah. He has always been and He will always be.

What Allah is like

  • Allah sees everything, hears everything and knows everything, even what is in our hearts.
  • Allah has power over all things. Nothing happens without His will.
  • Allah gives us food, health, family and every other blessing.
  • Nothing is like Allah, and we cannot see Him in this world.

What we do

  • We worship only Allah and ask only Him for help.
  • We thank Allah by saying "Alhamdulillah" and by obeying Him.

Level 1 · EasyThe Kalimah and the Five Pillars

The words that make a person Muslim, and the five duties Islam is built on.

The Kalimah

  • The first Kalimah is: "La ilaha illallah, Muhammadur Rasulullah."
  • It means: there is no god but Allah, and Muhammad (peace be upon him) is the Messenger of Allah.
  • Whoever believes this in the heart and says it with the tongue is a Muslim.

The five pillars of Islam

  • 1. Shahadah: bearing witness that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad (peace be upon him) is His Messenger.
  • 2. Salah: praying five times every day.
  • 3. Zakat: giving a fixed part of wealth to the poor each year.
  • 4. Sawm: fasting in the month of Ramadan.
  • 5. Hajj: travelling to Makkah for pilgrimage once in a lifetime, for those who are able.

Level 1 · EasyThe Six Kalimahs

The six short statements of faith that children memorise.

The first two

  • 1. Kalimah Tayyibah (the pure word): "La ilaha illallahu Muhammadur Rasulullah." There is no god but Allah, Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah.
  • 2. Kalimah Shahadah (the word of witness): "Ash-hadu al-la ilaha illallahu wahdahu la sharika lahu, wa ash-hadu anna Muhammadan abduhu wa rasuluh." I bear witness that there is no god but Allah, alone, with no partner, and I bear witness that Muhammad is His servant and Messenger.

The next four

  • 3. Kalimah Tamjeed (the word of glory): it praises Allah with "Subhanallah, walhamdulillah, wa la ilaha illallah, wallahu akbar".
  • 4. Kalimah Tawheed (the word of oneness): it says that Allah is One, He gives life and death, and He never dies.
  • 5. Kalimah Istighfar (the word of forgiveness): in it we ask Allah to forgive every sin, done knowingly or unknowingly, secretly or openly.
  • 6. Kalimah Radd-e-Kufr (the word of rejecting disbelief): in it we ask Allah to protect us from shirk and kufr and we turn away from them.

Iman in short (Iman Mujmal)

  • "I believe in Allah as He is, with His names and His qualities, and I accept all His commands."

Level 1 · EasyOur Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him)

The main events in the life of the last Messenger of Allah.

His family and childhood

  • He was born in Makkah, on a Monday in the month of Rabi al-Awwal, in the Year of the Elephant.
  • His father was Abdullah and his mother was Aminah.
  • His father passed away before he was born, and his mother passed away when he was six.
  • He was then cared for by his grandfather Abdul Muttalib, and after him by his uncle Abu Talib.
  • Even before prophethood, people called him As-Sadiq (the truthful) and Al-Amin (the trustworthy).

Prophethood

  • At the age of 40, the angel Jibril brought him the first revelation in the Cave of Hira.
  • The first word revealed was "Iqra", which means "Read".
  • He called people to Islam in Makkah for 13 years.
  • He then migrated to Madinah. This journey is called the Hijrah.
  • He lived in Madinah for 10 years and passed away there at the age of 63.

What we owe him

  • We love him more than anyone else in creation.
  • We follow his way, which is called the Sunnah.
  • When we hear his name, we say "Sallallahu alayhi wa sallam".
  • He is the last prophet. No prophet will come after him.

Level 1 · EasyThe Holy Quran

What the Quran is and how we respect it.

The book of Allah

  • The Quran is the word of Allah.
  • The angel Jibril brought it to Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him).
  • It was revealed little by little over about 23 years.
  • It is in the Arabic language, and not one letter of it has ever changed. Allah Himself protects it.

Facts to remember

  • The Quran has 114 surahs and 30 parts (juz).
  • The first surah is Al-Fatihah and the last is An-Nas.
  • The longest surah is Al-Baqarah and the shortest is Al-Kawthar.
  • A person who memorises the whole Quran is called a Hafiz.

Manners with the Quran

  • Make wudu before touching the Quran.
  • Say "A'udhu billahi minash-shaytanir-rajim" and "Bismillahir-Rahmanir-Rahim" before reciting.
  • Recite slowly and with tajweed.
  • Keep the Quran in a clean, high place.

Level 1 · EasyThe Five Daily Prayers

The names, times and rak'at of the five prayers.

Why we pray

  • Salah is the second pillar of Islam and is fard on every adult Muslim.
  • It is the first thing we will be asked about on the Day of Judgement.
  • Children should start praying at the age of seven.

The five prayers and their fard rak'at

  • Fajr: before sunrise. 2 fard.
  • Zuhr: after midday. 4 fard.
  • Asr: in the late afternoon. 4 fard.
  • Maghrib: just after sunset. 3 fard.
  • Isha: at night. 4 fard, and then 3 rak'at of Witr, which is wajib.

The sunnah prayers with them

  • Fajr: 2 sunnah before the fard.
  • Zuhr: 4 sunnah before and 2 sunnah after the fard.
  • Maghrib: 2 sunnah after the fard.
  • Isha: 2 sunnah after the fard, then Witr.

Level 1 · EasyAdhan and Iqamah

The call to prayer and how to answer it.

The adhan

  • The adhan is the call that tells Muslims the time of prayer has come. The one who calls it is the mu'adhdhin.
  • The first mu'adhdhin of Islam was Bilal (may Allah be pleased with him).
  • The words are: Allahu Akbar (4 times), Ash-hadu al-la ilaha illallah (2), Ash-hadu anna Muhammadar-Rasulullah (2), Hayya alas-salah (2), Hayya alal-falah (2), Allahu Akbar (2), La ilaha illallah (1).
  • In the Fajr adhan, "As-salatu khayrum minan-nawm" (prayer is better than sleep) is added twice after Hayya alal-falah.

The iqamah

  • The iqamah is called just before the fard prayer begins in congregation.
  • It has the same words as the adhan, with "Qad qamatis-salah" (the prayer has begun) added twice after Hayya alal-falah.

Answering the adhan

  • Stop talking and listen.
  • Repeat each phrase after the mu'adhdhin.
  • After "Hayya alas-salah" and "Hayya alal-falah", say "La hawla wa la quwwata illa billah".
  • When it ends, send durood on the Prophet (peace be upon him) and read the dua after the adhan.

Level 1 · EasyHow to Pray Two Rak'at

The order of a two-rak'ah prayer and what is recited in each position.

The first rak'ah

  • Stand facing the qiblah, make the intention, raise both hands to the ears and say "Allahu Akbar". Then fold the right hand over the left below the navel.
  • Recite the Thana: "Subhanakallahumma wa bihamdika..."
  • Recite "A'udhu billahi minash-shaytanir-rajim" and "Bismillahir-Rahmanir-Rahim".
  • Recite Surah Al-Fatihah, say "Ameen" quietly, then recite another surah.
  • Say "Allahu Akbar" and bow in ruku. Say "Subhana Rabbiyal-Azim" three times.
  • Stand up saying "Sami'allahu liman hamidah", then "Rabbana lakal-hamd".
  • Say "Allahu Akbar" and go into sajdah. Say "Subhana Rabbiyal-A'la" three times.
  • Sit up, then make the second sajdah in the same way.

The second rak'ah

  • Stand up and recite Bismillah, Surah Al-Fatihah and another surah. Then make ruku and the two sajdahs as before.
  • Sit and recite the Tashahhud (At-tahiyyat). Raise the right index finger at "Ash-hadu al-la ilaha".
  • Recite the Durood Ibrahim, then a dua.
  • Turn the face to the right and say "As-salamu alaykum wa rahmatullah", then to the left and say it again.

In sajdah, seven parts touch the ground

  • The forehead with the nose, both hands, both knees and the toes of both feet.

Women

  • A woman raises her hands only to the shoulders, places them on the chest, and keeps her body close together in ruku and sajdah.

Level 1 · EasyCleanliness

How a Muslim keeps the body, clothes and home clean.

Why it matters

  • The Prophet (peace be upon him) said that cleanliness is half of faith.
  • Allah loves those who keep themselves pure.

Using the toilet (istinja)

  • Enter with the left foot after saying the dua, and leave with the right foot.
  • Do not face the qiblah or turn your back to it.
  • Sit down, and do not talk.
  • Wash with the left hand until clean, then wash both hands with soap.

Every day

  • Brush the teeth. Using a miswak is a sunnah, especially before wudu and prayer.
  • Wash the hands before and after eating.
  • Keep clothes, the prayer place and the room tidy and clean.

Every week

  • Take a bath, especially on Friday.
  • Cut the nails and keep the hair neat.
  • Wear clean clothes for Jumu'ah.

Level 1 · EasyGood Manners Every Day

The manners of eating, greeting, and living with parents and teachers.

Eating and drinking

  • Wash your hands, sit down, and say "Bismillah" before eating.
  • Eat with the right hand and eat from what is in front of you.
  • Do not waste food and do not find fault with it.
  • Drink sitting down, in three breaths, not in one gulp.
  • Say "Alhamdulillah" when you finish.

Parents

  • Obey your parents in everything that is good.
  • Speak to them softly. Never say even "uff" to them.
  • Help them, thank them, and make dua for them.

Teachers and others

  • Greet your teacher with salam and listen carefully.
  • Say "As-salamu alaykum" when you meet a Muslim and reply "Wa alaykumus-salam".
  • Always tell the truth, keep your promises, and be kind to those younger than you.
  • Say "JazakAllahu khayran" to thank someone.

Going out and coming home

  • Say the dua when leaving the house and say salam when you come in.
  • Enter the toilet with the left foot and leave with the right foot.
  • Enter the masjid with the right foot and leave with the left foot.

Level 1 · EasyRespecting Parents, Teachers and Elders

The rights of the people who care for us.

Parents

  • After the worship of Allah, the Quran commands kindness to parents.
  • Come at once when they call, and answer politely.
  • Do not raise your voice, argue or walk in front of them rudely.
  • Do the work they ask before they ask twice.
  • Paradise lies under the feet of the mother.
  • Make this dua for them: "Rabbir-hamhuma kama rabbayani saghira."

Teachers

  • A teacher of the Quran and religion has a great right over the student.
  • Arrive on time, sit respectfully and listen without interrupting.
  • Do the homework and revise the lesson.
  • Make dua for your teachers.

Elders, relatives and neighbours

  • Greet elders first and give them your seat.
  • Visit relatives and keep good ties with them.
  • Do not trouble the neighbours with noise or mess. Share food with them.
  • Be gentle with those younger than you. The one who shows no mercy to the young and no respect to the old is not following the way of the Prophet (peace be upon him).

Level 1 · EasyThe Islamic Months and Special Days

The Hijri calendar and the days Muslims remember.

The Hijri calendar

  • The Islamic calendar follows the moon. A month has 29 or 30 days and begins when the new moon is seen.
  • It is counted from the Hijrah, the year the Prophet (peace be upon him) migrated to Madinah.
  • The twelve months are: Muharram, Safar, Rabi al-Awwal, Rabi al-Thani, Jumada al-Ula, Jumada al-Akhirah, Rajab, Sha'ban, Ramadan, Shawwal, Dhul Qa'dah and Dhul Hijjah.
  • Four months are sacred: Dhul Qa'dah, Dhul Hijjah, Muharram and Rajab.

Special days

  • Friday (Jumu'ah) is the best day of the week.
  • Ashura is the 10th of Muharram. Fasting on it, together with the 9th or the 11th, is a sunnah.
  • Ramadan is the ninth month, the month of fasting.
  • Eid al-Fitr is the 1st of Shawwal.
  • The Day of Arafah is the 9th of Dhul Hijjah, and Eid al-Adha is the 10th.

Level 2 · MediumThe Articles of Faith

The seven things every Muslim believes in (Iman Mufassal).

What a Muslim believes in

  • 1. Allah.
  • 2. His angels.
  • 3. His books.
  • 4. His messengers.
  • 5. The Last Day.
  • 6. Fate (taqdir): that all good and bad is by the decree of Allah.
  • 7. Life after death.

The Last Day

  • One day this whole world will end. Only Allah knows when.
  • Everyone will be brought back to life and will stand before Allah.
  • Every deed, big or small, will be shown and weighed.
  • Those whom Allah is pleased with will enter Jannah, and those who rejected Him will enter Jahannam.

Fate (taqdir)

  • Allah knew everything before it happened and it is all written.
  • We are still responsible for what we choose to do, so we must choose what is right.
  • When something hard happens, a Muslim is patient. When something good happens, a Muslim is thankful.

Level 2 · MediumThe Angels

Who the angels are and what they do.

About the angels

  • Angels are a creation of Allah made from light.
  • We cannot see them. They do not eat, drink or sleep.
  • They never disobey Allah. They do exactly what He commands.
  • Only Allah knows how many angels there are.

Four well-known angels

  • Jibril brought the revelation of Allah to the prophets.
  • Mikail is in charge of rain and provision.
  • Israfil will blow the trumpet on the Last Day.
  • The Angel of Death (Izrail) takes the souls at the time of death.

Other angels

  • Kiraman Katibin are two angels with every person. One writes the good deeds and the other writes the bad deeds.
  • Munkar and Nakir question a person in the grave.

Level 2 · MediumThe Books of Allah

The books Allah sent down and the prophets who received them.

The four great books

  • The Tawrah was given to Prophet Musa (peace be upon him).
  • The Zabur was given to Prophet Dawud (peace be upon him).
  • The Injil was given to Prophet Isa (peace be upon him).
  • The Quran was given to Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him).

What we believe

  • All these books were truly from Allah. Smaller scriptures were also given to other prophets, such as Ibrahim (peace be upon him).
  • The earlier books were changed by people over time.
  • The Quran is the last book. Allah has promised to protect it, and it will stay unchanged until the Last Day.
  • We follow the Quran, which confirms the truth that came before it.

Level 2 · MediumThe Prophets of Allah

Why Allah sent prophets and who some of them were.

About the prophets

  • Allah sent prophets to every nation to teach people to worship Him alone.
  • All prophets were human beings, and all were truthful and free from sin.
  • The first prophet was Adam and the last is Muhammad (peace be upon them).
  • The Quran mentions 25 prophets by name. Allah sent many more, and we believe in all of them.

Some prophets and their titles

  • Adam: Safiyullah, the chosen one of Allah.
  • Nuh: built the ark and was saved from the flood.
  • Ibrahim: Khalilullah, the close friend of Allah. He built the Ka'bah with his son Ismail.
  • Musa: Kalimullah, the one who spoke with Allah.
  • Isa: Ruhullah. He was born to Maryam without a father and was raised up to the heavens.
  • Muhammad: Khatamun-Nabiyyin, the Seal of the Prophets.

Miracles

  • A miracle (mu'jizah) is something amazing that Allah shows through a prophet to prove he is true.
  • The staff of Musa became a snake, and the sea opened for him.
  • The greatest miracle of our Prophet (peace be upon him) is the Quran.

Level 2 · MediumStories of the Prophets

Short accounts of some of the prophets named in the Quran.

Adam and Nuh

  • Adam was the first human and the first prophet. Allah made him from clay and taught him the names of all things.
  • Iblis refused to bow to Adam out of pride and became the enemy of mankind.
  • Nuh called his people for 950 years. Only a few believed. Allah told him to build an ark, and the believers were saved from the flood.

Ibrahim and Ismail

  • Ibrahim broke the idols of his people. They threw him into a fire, and Allah made it cool and safe for him.
  • He left his wife Hajar and baby Ismail in the valley of Makkah by the command of Allah. The well of Zamzam sprang up for them.
  • He was ready to sacrifice his son for Allah, and Allah sent a ram in his place. We remember this at Eid al-Adha.

Yusuf and Yunus

  • Yusuf was thrown into a well by his jealous brothers. After years of patience he became a minister of Egypt and forgave them.
  • Yunus was swallowed by a great fish. He called: "La ilaha illa anta subhanaka inni kuntu minaz-zalimin", and Allah saved him.

Musa and Isa

  • Musa was sent to Firawn, who claimed to be a god. Allah parted the sea for Musa and his people, and Firawn was drowned.
  • His brother Harun was also a prophet and helped him.
  • Isa was born to Maryam without a father. He spoke as a baby in the cradle.
  • By the permission of Allah he healed the blind and the sick and gave life to the dead.
  • He was not killed. Allah raised him up, and he will return before the Last Day.

Level 2 · MediumThe Prophet's Life in Makkah

From the first revelation to the Hijrah.

The message begins

  • After the first revelation, the Prophet (peace be upon him) invited people quietly for three years.
  • The first to believe were his wife Khadijah, his friend Abu Bakr, his young cousin Ali and his freed servant Zayd (may Allah be pleased with them).
  • Then he was commanded to call people openly, and he stood on Mount Safa and warned the Quraysh.

Hardship

  • The Quraysh mocked and hurt the Muslims. Bilal was tortured on the hot sand and kept saying "Ahad, Ahad" (One, One).
  • Some Muslims migrated to Abyssinia, where a just Christian king gave them safety.
  • The Muslims and the family of the Prophet were boycotted in a valley for about three years.
  • In one year both his uncle Abu Talib and his wife Khadijah passed away. It is called the Year of Sorrow.
  • He went to Taif to invite its people. They stoned him, yet he made dua for them and not against them.

Isra and Mi'raj

  • In one night Allah took him from Makkah to Masjid al-Aqsa in Jerusalem, and from there up through the heavens.
  • On this journey the five daily prayers were made fard.

The Hijrah

  • The people of Madinah accepted Islam and invited him.
  • He left Makkah with Abu Bakr and they hid in the Cave of Thawr for three nights.
  • He said to Abu Bakr: "Do not grieve. Allah is with us."
  • They reached Madinah safely. The Islamic calendar begins from this year.

Level 2 · MediumThe Prophet's Life in Madinah

The ten years in which the Muslim community was built.

A new home

  • On the way he built Masjid Quba, the first masjid of Islam. In Madinah he built Masjid an-Nabawi.
  • He made the migrants (Muhajirun) and the helpers of Madinah (Ansar) brothers to one another.
  • The qiblah was changed from Jerusalem to the Ka'bah in Makkah.

Main events

  • Battle of Badr, 2 AH: 313 Muslims defeated an army about three times larger, with the help of Allah.
  • Battle of Uhud, 3 AH: the Muslims suffered a setback when some archers left their place. It taught the lesson of obedience.
  • Battle of the Trench (Khandaq), 5 AH: a trench was dug around Madinah on the advice of Salman al-Farisi.
  • Treaty of Hudaybiyyah, 6 AH: a peace treaty that the Quran called a clear victory.
  • Conquest of Makkah, 8 AH: he entered Makkah without a battle, forgave his enemies and cleared the Ka'bah of idols.
  • Farewell Hajj, 10 AH: he gave his last sermon, saying that all people are equal and that he was leaving behind the Quran and his Sunnah.

His passing

  • He passed away in Madinah in Rabi al-Awwal, 11 AH, at the age of 63.
  • He is buried in Madinah, in the room of Aishah (may Allah be pleased with her), next to his masjid.

Level 2 · MediumThe Sahabah

The companions of the Prophet (peace be upon him).

Who is a Sahabi?

  • A Sahabi is a person who met the Prophet (peace be upon him) as a Muslim and died as a Muslim.
  • The Sahabah are the best people of this ummah. Allah is pleased with them.
  • When we say the name of a Sahabi we add "radiyallahu anhu", which means "may Allah be pleased with him".
  • We love and respect all of them and never speak badly of any of them.

The four rightly guided Khalifahs

  • 1. Abu Bakr As-Siddiq: the closest friend of the Prophet and his companion in the Hijrah.
  • 2. Umar Al-Faruq: known for his justice.
  • 3. Uthman Dhun-Nurayn: known for his modesty and generosity.
  • 4. Ali Al-Murtada: the cousin and son-in-law of the Prophet, known for his knowledge and courage.

The family of the Prophet

  • His wives are called the Mothers of the Believers. The first was Khadijah.
  • His daughters were Zaynab, Ruqayyah, Umm Kulthum and Fatimah.
  • His sons Qasim, Abdullah and Ibrahim all passed away in childhood.
  • Hasan and Husayn were his grandsons, the sons of Fatimah and Ali.

Level 2 · MediumThe Four Khalifahs in Detail

The leaders of the Muslims after the Prophet (peace be upon him).

Abu Bakr As-Siddiq

  • The first adult man to accept Islam and the closest friend of the Prophet.
  • He was called As-Siddiq because he believed in the Mi'raj without any doubt.
  • He was Khalifah for about two years. In his time the Quran was gathered into one book.

Umar Al-Faruq

  • His acceptance of Islam gave the Muslims strength to pray openly.
  • He was Khalifah for about ten years. Islam spread to Iraq, Syria, Egypt and Persia, and Jerusalem was opened.
  • He began the Hijri calendar and used to walk the streets at night to check on the poor.

Uthman Dhun-Nurayn

  • He married two daughters of the Prophet, one after the other, so he is called "the one with two lights".
  • He bought the well of Rumah and gave it to the Muslims for free.
  • He was Khalifah for about twelve years. He had copies of the Quran written and sent to every region.

Ali Al-Murtada

  • He accepted Islam as a child and was raised in the home of the Prophet.
  • He slept in the bed of the Prophet on the night of the Hijrah.
  • He married Fatimah, the daughter of the Prophet. He was Khalifah for about five years and was known for his knowledge and bravery.

The ten promised Paradise

  • Ten companions were given the good news of Paradise in one statement. They include the four Khalifahs, Talhah, Zubayr, Abdur-Rahman ibn Awf, Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, Sa'id ibn Zayd and Abu Ubaydah (may Allah be pleased with them all).

Level 2 · MediumLife After Death

What happens from the grave to Paradise.

The grave

  • After death, the soul stays in a world called Barzakh until the Day of Rising.
  • Two angels, Munkar and Nakir, ask three questions: Who is your Lord? What is your religion? Who is your Prophet?
  • The believer answers: My Lord is Allah, my religion is Islam, and my Prophet is Muhammad (peace be upon him).
  • The grave is made wide and peaceful for the believer.

The Day of Judgement

  • Israfil will blow the trumpet. Everything will end, and at the second blowing everyone will rise.
  • Each person will be given a book of deeds. The successful receive it in the right hand.
  • Deeds will be weighed on the Scale (Mizan).
  • Everyone will cross the Bridge (Sirat) that is set over Jahannam.
  • The Prophet (peace be upon him) will give his ummah water from his pool, Al-Kawthar, and will intercede for them by the permission of Allah.

Paradise and the Fire

  • Jannah is the home of everlasting happiness for the believers. Its greatest blessing is seeing Allah.
  • Jahannam is the place of punishment for those who rejected Allah.
  • A person with faith in the heart will, in the end, enter Jannah.

Level 2 · MediumPurity: Wudu, Ghusl and Tayammum

How a Muslim becomes pure for worship.

Two kinds of impurity

  • Visible impurity (najasah haqiqi) is something unclean that can be seen, such as urine or blood. It is removed by washing.
  • Unseen impurity (najasah hukmi) is the state of being without wudu or needing ghusl. It is removed by wudu or ghusl.

Wudu and ghusl

  • Wudu has 4 fard: washing the face, washing both arms including the elbows, wiping a quarter of the head, and washing both feet including the ankles.
  • Ghusl has 3 fard: rinsing the mouth, taking water into the nose, and washing the whole body.
  • The full steps are in the Wudu and Ghusl guides in Free Resources.

Tayammum

  • Tayammum is purifying with clean earth when water cannot be found or cannot be used because of illness.
  • It has 3 fard: making the intention, striking the hands on clean earth and wiping the whole face, then striking again and wiping both arms including the elbows.
  • Whatever breaks wudu also breaks tayammum. Finding water breaks it too.

Level 2 · MediumSalah in Detail

The conditions before salah and the fard acts inside it.

The 7 conditions before salah

  • 1. The body is pure.
  • 2. The clothes are pure.
  • 3. The place of prayer is pure.
  • 4. The satr is covered. For a man this is from the navel to below the knees. A woman covers her whole body except the face, the hands and the feet.
  • 5. The time of that prayer has started.
  • 6. Facing the qiblah.
  • 7. Making the intention (niyyah) for that prayer.

The 6 fard acts of salah

  • 1. Takbir tahrimah: saying "Allahu Akbar" to begin.
  • 2. Qiyam: standing.
  • 3. Qira'ah: reciting from the Quran.
  • 4. Ruku: bowing.
  • 5. Sujud: the two prostrations in each rak'ah.
  • 6. The last sitting (qa'dah akhirah), for as long as it takes to recite the tashahhud.

Remember

  • If a condition or a fard act is missed, the salah is not valid and must be prayed again.
25

Level 2 · MediumThe Times of Prayer

When each prayer begins and ends, and when prayer is not allowed.

The five times

  • Fajr: from true dawn until just before sunrise.
  • Zuhr: from when the sun passes its highest point until the shadow of an object is twice its length, apart from its midday shadow.
  • Asr: from the end of Zuhr until sunset. It is disliked to delay it until the sun turns pale.
  • Maghrib: from sunset until the red and white glow leaves the sky.
  • Isha: from the end of Maghrib until true dawn. It is best prayed before midnight. Witr is prayed after Isha.

Three times when no prayer is allowed

  • While the sun is rising.
  • When the sun is at its highest point at midday.
  • While the sun is setting. Only the Asr of that same day may be prayed then.

Two times when nafl is not prayed

  • After the Fajr prayer until the sun has risen.
  • After the Asr prayer until the sun has set.
26

Level 2 · MediumFasting and Ramadan

The fourth pillar of Islam and the blessed month.

What fasting is

  • Fasting (sawm) means not eating, drinking or doing anything that breaks the fast, from true dawn until sunset, with the intention of worship.
  • Fasting in Ramadan is fard on every adult, sane Muslim.
  • A sick person or a traveller may delay the fast and make it up later.

During the fast

  • Eating the pre-dawn meal (suhur) is sunnah and full of blessing.
  • Break the fast as soon as the sun sets, preferably with dates or water.
  • Eating or drinking by mistake, forgetting that you are fasting, does not break the fast.
  • Eating or drinking on purpose breaks the fast and is a serious sin in Ramadan.
  • A fasting person keeps away from lying, backbiting and fighting.

The blessings of Ramadan

  • The Quran was first revealed in Ramadan.
  • Tarawih is 20 rak'at prayed after Isha every night of Ramadan.
  • Laylatul Qadr is better than a thousand months. We look for it in the odd nights of the last ten.
  • I'tikaf is staying in the masjid for worship in the last ten days.
  • Sadaqatul Fitr is given to the poor before the Eid prayer.
27

Level 2 · MediumDhikr, Durood and Short Surahs

Words of remembrance every Muslim should know.

Words of dhikr

  • Subhanallah: Allah is free from every fault.
  • Alhamdulillah: all praise is for Allah.
  • Allahu Akbar: Allah is the Greatest.
  • La ilaha illallah: there is no god but Allah. It is the best dhikr.
  • Astaghfirullah: I ask Allah for forgiveness.
  • InshaAllah: if Allah wills. We say it when we plan to do something.
  • MashaAllah: this is what Allah willed. We say it when we see something good.

After every fard prayer

  • Subhanallah 33 times, Alhamdulillah 33 times and Allahu Akbar 34 times. This is called Tasbih Fatimi.
  • Ayat al-Kursi (Al-Baqarah 2:255).

Durood

  • Durood is asking Allah to send His blessings on the Prophet (peace be upon him).
  • Whoever sends one durood, Allah sends ten mercies on that person.
  • The best durood is Durood Ibrahim, which we recite in salah. Send plenty of durood on Friday.

Surahs to memorise first

  • Al-Fatihah, then the last ten surahs of the Quran, from Al-Fil to An-Nas.
  • Al-Ikhlas, Al-Falaq and An-Nas are recited for protection every morning and evening and before sleeping.
28

Level 3 · AdvancedThe Types of Rulings

What fard, wajib, sunnah and the other terms mean.

Things we are asked to do

  • Fard: proven beyond doubt and compulsory. Leaving it without a valid reason is a major sin, and denying it takes a person out of Islam.
  • Wajib: necessary. Leaving it on purpose is a sin, but denying it is not disbelief.
  • Sunnah mu'akkadah: what the Prophet (peace be upon him) did regularly. Leaving it as a habit is blameworthy.
  • Sunnah ghayr mu'akkadah and mustahab: rewarded if done, with no sin if left.

Things that are allowed

  • Mubah (halal): allowed, with no reward and no sin in itself.

Things we are asked to avoid

  • Haram: clearly forbidden. Doing it is a major sin.
  • Makruh tahrimi: close to haram. Doing it is a sin.
  • Makruh tanzihi: disliked. Avoiding it is better and is rewarded.

Two kinds of fard

  • Fard ayn must be done by every single person, such as the five prayers.
  • Fard kifayah is fulfilled for everyone when some people do it, such as the funeral prayer.
29

Level 3 · AdvancedImpurity and Clean Water

The kinds of impurity and which water can be used for purity.

Heavy impurity (najasah ghalizah)

  • Examples: human urine and stool, flowing blood, wine, and the droppings of animals that are not eaten.
  • If it is solid, an amount up to about the weight of a dirham is excused. If it is liquid, an area up to the size of the hollow of the palm is excused. More than that must be washed before praying.

Light impurity (najasah khafifah)

  • Examples: the urine of animals whose meat is halal, such as cows and goats, and the droppings of birds that are not eaten.
  • It is excused if it covers less than a quarter of the garment or limb.

How to clean

  • Wash the place three times and squeeze the cloth each time, or wash under running water until the impurity is gone.
  • A hard, smooth surface such as glass or a tile becomes clean when it is wiped until nothing remains.

Water for wudu and ghusl

  • Clean and usable: rain water, well and spring water, river and sea water, and melted snow.
  • Not usable: water whose colour, taste or smell has changed because of an impurity, and water already used for wudu or ghusl.
  • A small amount of still water becomes impure if any impurity falls into it.
  • Flowing water and a very large body of water stay clean unless the impurity changes the colour, taste or smell.
30

Level 3 · AdvancedWajib Acts of Salah and Sajdah Sahw

The necessary acts of salah, what breaks it, and how mistakes are corrected.

Some of the wajib acts of salah

  • Reciting Surah Al-Fatihah.
  • Reciting a surah or some verses after Al-Fatihah in the first two rak'at of a fard prayer, and in every rak'ah of witr, sunnah and nafl.
  • Standing up straight after ruku (qawmah) and sitting between the two sajdahs (jalsah).
  • Doing every posture calmly, without rushing.
  • The first sitting (qa'dah ula) in a prayer of three or four rak'at.
  • Reciting the tashahhud in both sittings.
  • Ending the prayer with the words of salam.
  • Reciting the dua of qunut in Witr.

Sajdah sahw

  • If a wajib act is left out by mistake, or a fard act is delayed, the prayer is corrected by sajdah sahw.
  • In the last sitting, after the tashahhud, say one salam to the right, make two sajdahs, then recite the tashahhud, durood and dua again and finish with both salams.
  • If a wajib is left out on purpose, sajdah sahw does not fix it. The prayer must be repeated.

What breaks the salah

  • Talking, even one word, on purpose or by mistake.
  • Eating or drinking.
  • Laughing out loud.
  • Turning the chest away from the qiblah without a reason.
  • A lot of extra movement that makes it look as if the person is not praying.
  • Anything that breaks wudu.
31

Level 3 · AdvancedCongregation, Travel and Missed Prayers

Praying behind an imam, shortening prayers on a journey, and making up missed prayers.

Praying in congregation (jama'ah)

  • Praying the fard prayers in congregation is sunnah mu'akkadah for men and carries twenty-seven times the reward.
  • The imam stands in front. The followers stand behind him in straight rows, shoulder to shoulder.
  • The follower does not recite Surah Al-Fatihah or a surah behind the imam. He stays silent and listens.
  • A person who joins late (masbuq) stands up after the imam's salam and completes what was missed.
  • Whoever reaches the ruku with the imam has caught that rak'ah.

The traveller (musafir)

  • A person who sets out to travel about 48 miles (about 77 km) or more is a traveller once outside his town.
  • The traveller prays two rak'at in place of the four fard of Zuhr, Asr and Isha. This is called qasr and it is wajib.
  • Fajr, Maghrib and Witr are not shortened.
  • If he intends to stay in one place for fifteen days or more, he prays in full.

Missed prayers (qada)

  • Missing a prayer without a valid reason is a major sin. Sleep and forgetting are valid reasons.
  • A missed fard prayer and Witr must be made up as soon as possible.
  • A person who has fewer than six missed prayers should pray them in order before the current prayer.
32

Level 3 · AdvancedJumu'ah, Eid and Other Prayers

Prayers besides the five daily ones.

Jumu'ah (Friday prayer)

  • Jumu'ah is fard on adult Muslim men who are resident and healthy. It takes the place of Zuhr.
  • It has 2 fard rak'at prayed in congregation after the khutbah.
  • Listening to the khutbah silently is wajib.
  • On Friday we take a bath, wear clean clothes, go early, and send plenty of durood.

The two Eid prayers

  • Eid al-Fitr comes after Ramadan and Eid al-Adha comes on the 10th of Dhul Hijjah.
  • Each Eid prayer is 2 rak'at with six extra takbirs, and it is wajib.
  • The khutbah of Eid comes after the prayer.

Other prayers

  • Witr: 3 rak'at after Isha. It is wajib.
  • Tarawih: 20 rak'at in the nights of Ramadan. It is sunnah mu'akkadah.
  • Janazah: the funeral prayer. It is fard kifayah, has four takbirs, and has no ruku or sajdah.
  • Tahajjud: a nafl prayer in the last part of the night with great reward.
33

Level 3 · AdvancedFasting: The Detailed Rulings

The kinds of fast, what breaks it, and how it is made up.

Kinds of fast

  • Fard: the fasts of Ramadan.
  • Wajib: a fast that a person has vowed to keep.
  • Sunnah and nafl: the 9th and 10th of Muharram, the Day of Arafah, six days of Shawwal, the 13th, 14th and 15th of each month, and Mondays and Thursdays.
  • Forbidden: fasting on the two days of Eid and on the 11th, 12th and 13th of Dhul Hijjah.

What does not break the fast

  • Eating or drinking out of forgetfulness.
  • Using a miswak, applying oil or kohl, and smelling perfume.
  • Vomiting without intending to.
  • Swallowing one's own saliva.

What breaks the fast

  • If a person eats or drinks on purpose in a Ramadan fast, both qada and kaffarah are due.
  • If water goes down the throat by mistake while rinsing the mouth, or a person eats thinking the time has not started or has already ended, only qada is due.

Qada, kaffarah and fidyah

  • Qada: keeping one fast in place of each fast missed or broken.
  • Kaffarah: fasting sixty days in a row. If a person truly cannot, sixty poor people are fed two meals.
  • Fidyah: a very old or permanently ill person who cannot fast gives food to one poor person for each fast, equal to Sadaqatul Fitr.

Sadaqatul Fitr

  • It is wajib on every Muslim who owns the nisab, for himself and his young children.
  • It is given before the Eid prayer so that the poor can also enjoy Eid.
34

Level 3 · AdvancedZakat

The third pillar of Islam: the right of the poor in our wealth.

What zakat is

  • Zakat is giving a fixed part of one's wealth to those who are entitled to it, for the sake of Allah.
  • It is fard on every adult, sane Muslim who owns the nisab and has had it for one full lunar year.
  • The amount is one fortieth, which is 2.5 percent.

The nisab

  • The nisab is the smallest amount of wealth on which zakat is due.
  • It is 87.48 grams of gold (7.5 tola) or 612.36 grams of silver (52.5 tola), or cash and trade goods of that value.
  • Zakat is paid on gold, silver, cash and goods kept for trade. It is not paid on one's home, clothes or things of daily use.

Who can receive it

  • The poor and the needy, people in debt, and travellers who are cut off from their wealth.
  • Zakat cannot be given to one's parents, grandparents, children, grandchildren, husband or wife.
  • It also cannot be given to a person who already owns the nisab.
  • The person who receives it must be made its owner.
35

Level 3 · AdvancedQurbani (Sacrifice)

The sacrifice of Eid al-Adha.

What it is

  • Qurbani is sacrificing an animal for Allah on the days of Eid al-Adha, in memory of Prophet Ibrahim (peace be upon him).
  • It is wajib on every adult, sane, resident Muslim who owns the nisab on those days.
  • Its time is from after the Eid prayer on the 10th of Dhul Hijjah until sunset on the 12th.

The animals

  • A goat or sheep: at least one year old. It counts for one person.
  • A cow or buffalo: at least two years old. Up to seven people may share it.
  • A camel: at least five years old. Up to seven people may share it.
  • The animal must be healthy and free from clear defects such as blindness or lameness.

The meat

  • It is best to divide it into three parts: one for the family, one for relatives and friends, and one for the poor.
  • Nothing of the animal may be given to the butcher as his wage.

The takbir of tashriq

  • From Fajr on the 9th of Dhul Hijjah until Asr on the 13th, this takbir is said once aloud after every fard prayer: "Allahu Akbar, Allahu Akbar, la ilaha illallahu wallahu Akbar, Allahu Akbar wa lillahil-hamd."
36

Level 3 · AdvancedHajj

The fifth pillar of Islam: the pilgrimage to Makkah.

Who must go

  • Hajj is fard once in a lifetime on every adult, sane Muslim who can afford the journey and is able to travel.
  • It takes place from the 8th to the 12th of Dhul Hijjah.

The 3 fard acts of Hajj

  • 1. Ihram: making the intention and saying the talbiyah.
  • 2. Wuquf at Arafat: staying in the plain of Arafat on the 9th of Dhul Hijjah.
  • 3. Tawaf az-Ziyarah: going seven times around the Ka'bah after Arafat.

Some of the wajib acts

  • Staying at Muzdalifah.
  • Sa'i: walking seven times between Safa and Marwah.
  • Stoning the pillars (jamarat) at Mina.
  • Shaving or shortening the hair.
  • The farewell tawaf for those who come from outside Makkah.

Words to know

  • Talbiyah: "Labbayk Allahumma labbayk", which means "Here I am, O Allah, here I am".
  • Tawaf: going around the Ka'bah seven times.
  • Umrah: the smaller pilgrimage, which can be done at any time of the year.
  • Eid al-Adha: the 10th of Dhul Hijjah, when an animal is sacrificed.
37

Level 3 · AdvancedHalal and Haram in Daily Life

Food, earning and everyday choices.

Food and drink

  • Halal means allowed and haram means forbidden.
  • Haram to eat: pork, blood, an animal that died by itself, and an animal slaughtered in a name other than Allah's.
  • Wine and everything that intoxicates are haram.
  • The meat of an animal is halal only if it is a halal animal slaughtered in the Islamic way, with the name of Allah.
  • Fish is halal without slaughter.
  • If we are unsure about a food, we leave it until we find out.

Earning

  • A Muslim earns through honest work and trade.
  • Stealing, cheating, bribery and gambling are haram.
  • Interest (riba) is haram, whether taking it or paying it.
  • Weighing and measuring must be full and fair.

Everyday conduct

  • Lying, backbiting, carrying tales and mocking others are haram.
  • Dressing modestly and lowering the gaze are part of faith.
  • A dua is more likely to be accepted when the food and income are halal.
38

Level 3 · AdvancedSins, Repentance and Good Character

What harms our faith and how to return to Allah.

The greatest sins

  • Shirk is to make anyone a partner with Allah in His being, His qualities or His worship. It is the greatest sin of all.
  • Kufr is to reject any part of what must be believed in Islam.
  • Other major sins include disobeying parents, lying, backbiting, stealing, missing the prayer and taking what belongs to others.
  • Bid'ah is adding something to the religion that has no basis in it.

Repentance (tawbah)

  • Allah forgives every sin for the one who truly repents.
  • True repentance means stopping the sin, feeling sorry for it, and firmly deciding never to return to it.
  • If the sin involved another person's right, that right must be returned or forgiveness asked from that person.
  • We say "Astaghfirullah", which means "I ask Allah for forgiveness".

Good character

  • Truthfulness, trustworthiness, patience, thankfulness and humility.
  • Being kind to parents, relatives, neighbours and the poor.
  • Controlling anger and forgiving others.
  • The Prophet (peace be upon him) had the best character of all people.
39

Level 3 · AdvancedSigns of the Last Day

What the Prophet (peace be upon him) told us will happen before the Hour.

What we believe

  • Only Allah knows when the Last Day will come.
  • The Prophet (peace be upon him) told us of signs that will appear before it, so that we stay ready.

Some of the smaller signs

  • Knowledge of religion will decrease and ignorance will spread.
  • Trust will be lost and lying will become common.
  • People will compete in building tall buildings.
  • Time will seem to pass quickly.

Some of the greater signs

  • The coming of Imam Mahdi, who will fill the earth with justice.
  • The appearance of the Dajjal, the great deceiver. We seek protection from his trial in every prayer.
  • The descent of Prophet Isa (peace be upon him), who will defeat the Dajjal.
  • The release of Yajuj and Majuj.
  • The sun rising from the west. After that, repentance will no longer be accepted.

What we should do

  • Hold firmly to the Quran and the Sunnah.
  • Recite the first ten verses of Surah Al-Kahf for protection from the Dajjal.
  • Hurry to repent and do good while there is time.
