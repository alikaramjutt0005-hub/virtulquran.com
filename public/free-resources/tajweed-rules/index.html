Level 1 · EasyWhat Is Tajweed?

What tajweed means and how we begin reciting.

Meaning

  • Tajweed means reciting every letter of the Quran correctly: from its own place in the mouth, with its own qualities.
  • The Quran was revealed with tajweed, and the Prophet (peace be upon him) recited it this way.
  • Learning enough tajweed to avoid clear mistakes is necessary for everyone who recites.

Two kinds of mistake

  • A clear mistake (lahn jali) changes a letter or a vowel, for example reading one letter in place of another. It can change the meaning and must be avoided.
  • A hidden mistake (lahn khafi) is leaving out a rule that makes the recitation beautiful, such as ghunnah or madd. It should also be avoided.

Before reciting

  • Begin with ta'awwudh: "A'udhu billahi minash-shaytanir-rajim".
  • Then say the basmalah: "Bismillahir-Rahmanir-Rahim".
  • The basmalah is recited at the start of every surah except Surah At-Tawbah.

Examples

  • اَعُوْذُ بِاللّٰهِ مِنَ الشَّیْطٰنِ الرَّجِیْمِTa'awwudh
  • بِسْمِ اللّٰهِ الرَّحْمٰنِ الرَّحِیْمِBasmalah

Level 1 · EasyMakharij: Where Letters Come From

The five areas of the mouth and throat that produce the Arabic letters.

The five areas

  • 1. The empty space of the mouth and throat (jawf): the three madd letters, alif, waw and ya.
  • 2. The throat (halq): six letters.
  • 3. The tongue (lisan): eighteen letters.
  • 4. The lips (shafatan): four letters.
  • 5. The nose (khayshum): the sound of ghunnah.
  • Together these areas have seventeen exact points of articulation.

The throat letters

  • From the bottom of the throat: hamzah and ha.
  • From the middle of the throat: ayn and ha (the sharp one).
  • From the top of the throat: ghayn and kha.

The lip letters

  • Fa: the top front teeth touch the inside of the bottom lip.
  • Ba and meem: the two lips close.
  • Waw: the two lips make a circle without closing.

How to find a makhraj

  • Put a sukun on the letter and a letter with fatha before it, then say it. The place where the sound stops is its makhraj.

Examples

  • ء هBottom of the throat
  • ع حMiddle of the throat
  • غ خTop of the throat
  • ف ب م وThe lips

Level 1 · EasyHeavy and Light Letters

Which letters are read with a full mouth and which are read thin.

Heavy letters (tafkhim)

  • Seven letters are always read heavy, with the back of the tongue raised: kha, saad, daad, ghayn, taa, qaaf and zaa.
  • They are remembered with the phrase "khussa daghtin qiz".

Light letters (tarqiq)

  • All the other letters are read light, with the tongue low and the mouth not full.

Three letters that change

  • Alif follows the letter before it: heavy after a heavy letter and light after a light letter.
  • The laam in the name "Allah" is heavy after fatha or damma and light after kasra.
  • Raa is sometimes heavy and sometimes light. It has its own chapter.

Examples

  • خ ص ض غ ط ق ظThe seven heavy letters
  • قَالَHeavy alif after qaaf
  • كَانَLight alif after kaaf

Level 2 · MediumNoon Sakinah and Tanween

The four rules for a noon with sukun and for tanween.

First, what they are

  • Noon sakinah is a noon with a sukun on it.
  • Tanween is the double fatha, double kasra or double damma at the end of a word. It sounds like a noon sakinah.
  • The rule depends on the letter that comes next.

1. Izhar (clear)

  • If one of the six throat letters comes next, the noon is read clearly without ghunnah.
  • The letters are: hamzah, ha, ayn, ha, ghayn, kha.

2. Idgham (merging)

  • If one of the six letters of "yarmaloon" comes next, the noon merges into that letter.
  • With ghunnah for four letters: ya, noon, meem, waw.
  • Without ghunnah for two letters: laam and raa.
  • Idgham only happens between two words, never inside one word.

3. Iqlab (changing)

  • If ba comes next, the noon changes into a meem and is read with ghunnah. A small meem is written above it.

4. Ikhfa (hiding)

  • If any of the remaining fifteen letters comes next, the noon is hidden: read between izhar and idgham, with ghunnah for two counts.

Examples

  • مَنْ اٰمَنَIzhar
  • مَنْ یَّقُوْلُIdgham with ghunnah
  • مِنْ رَّبِّهِمْIdgham without ghunnah
  • مِنْۢ بَعْدِIqlab
  • مِنْ قَبْلُIkhfa

Level 2 · MediumMeem Sakinah

The three rules for a meem with sukun.

1. Ikhfa shafawi

  • If ba comes after a meem sakinah, the meem is hidden with ghunnah, with the lips closed lightly.

2. Idgham shafawi

  • If another meem comes after it, the two meems merge into one with ghunnah.

3. Izhar shafawi

  • Before every other letter, the meem is read clearly without ghunnah.
  • Take extra care to keep it clear before waw and fa.

Examples

  • تَرْمِیْهِمْ بِحِجَارَةٍIkhfa shafawi
  • لَهُمْ مَّاIdgham shafawi
  • اَلَمْ تَرَIzhar shafawi

Level 2 · MediumGhunnah

The sound from the nose on noon and meem.

What it is

  • Ghunnah is a soft sound that comes from the nose.
  • It belongs to the letters noon and meem.

When it is held

  • When noon or meem has a shaddah, ghunnah is held for two counts. This is always done.
  • Ghunnah is also held in ikhfa, iqlab and idgham with ghunnah.

How long

  • One count is about the time it takes to close or open a finger.
  • Two counts is the same length as a normal madd.

Examples

  • اِنَّNoon with shaddah
  • ثُمَّMeem with shaddah

Level 2 · MediumQalqalah

The bouncing sound on five letters.

The letters

  • Qalqalah is a slight bounce in the sound when one of five letters has a sukun.
  • The letters are qaaf, taa, ba, jeem and daal, remembered as "qutbu jad".

Two strengths

  • Smaller qalqalah: the letter has a sukun in the middle of a word or the middle of recitation.
  • Greater qalqalah: we stop on the letter at the end of a word. The bounce is stronger.
  • If the last letter also has a shaddah, the bounce is strongest.

Take care

  • Do not add a vowel to the letter. It is a bounce, not a fatha or a kasra.

Examples

  • ق ط ب ج دThe five letters
  • یَدْخُلُوْنَSmaller qalqalah
  • اَحَدٌGreater qalqalah when stopping
  • الْفَلَقِGreater qalqalah when stopping

Level 2 · MediumSifaat: The Qualities of Letters

How each letter sounds once it leaves its makhraj.

What sifaat are

  • The makhraj tells us where a letter comes from. Its sifaat tell us how it sounds.
  • Two letters can share a makhraj and differ only in their qualities, such as taa (ط) and taa (ت).

Qualities that have an opposite

  • Hams (breath flows) and jahr (breath is held). The ten letters of hams are in the phrase "fahaththahu shakhsun sakat".
  • Shiddah (sound stops), tawassut (in between) and rakhawah (sound flows). The eight letters of shiddah are in "ajid qatin bakat", and the five of tawassut are in "lin umar".
  • Isti'la (tongue rises, heavy) and istifal (tongue stays low, light). The seven letters of isti'la are in "khussa daghtin qiz".
  • Itbaq (tongue presses against the roof of the mouth) and infitah (it stays apart). The four letters of itbaq are saad, daad, taa and zaa.
  • Idhlaq (said easily from the tip of the tongue or lips) and ismat. The six letters of idhlaq are in "farra min lubb".

Qualities with no opposite

  • Safir: a whistling sound in saad, zay and seen.
  • Qalqalah: a bounce in the letters of "qutbu jad".
  • Leen: softness in waw and ya sakinah after a fatha.
  • Inhiraf: the sound leans aside in laam and raa.
  • Takrir: the tongue trembles in raa. It must be controlled so that raa is not repeated.
  • Tafashshi: the sound spreads across the mouth in sheen.
  • Istitalah: the sound stretches along the side of the tongue in daad.

Examples

  • ص ز سSafir: whistling
  • ص ض ط ظThe four letters of itbaq
  • شTafashshi: spreading
  • ضIstitalah: stretching

Level 3 · AdvancedMadd: Lengthening

The madd letters and how long each kind is held.

The madd letters

  • Alif after a fatha.
  • Waw sakinah after a damma.
  • Ya sakinah after a kasra.
  • In the IndoPak script, the standing fatha, standing kasra and inverted damma are also read as madd for two counts.

Natural madd (madd asli)

  • When no hamzah or sukun follows the madd letter, it is held for two counts. No more and no less.

Madd because of hamzah

  • Madd muttasil: the hamzah is in the same word. It must be lengthened, for four or five counts.
  • Madd munfasil: the hamzah is at the start of the next word. It is lengthened, for four or five counts.

Madd because of sukun

  • Madd lazim: a permanent sukun or shaddah follows. It is always six counts.
  • Madd arid: the sukun appears only because we stop. It may be two, four or six counts.
  • Madd leen: waw or ya sakinah after a fatha, when we stop on the next letter. It may be two, four or six counts.

Examples

  • قَالَNatural madd, 2 counts
  • جَآءَMadd muttasil
  • یٰۤاَیُّهَاMadd munfasil
  • الضَّآلِّیْنَMadd lazim, 6 counts
  • نَسْتَعِیْنُMadd arid when stopping
  • خَوْفٍMadd leen when stopping

Level 3 · AdvancedMore Kinds of Madd

Madd badal, madd iwad, madd silah and the four kinds of madd lazim.

Madd badal

  • A hamzah comes before the madd letter in the same word. It is held for two counts.

Madd iwad

  • When we stop on a double fatha, it becomes an alif held for two counts. This does not apply to the round ta (ة).

Madd silah

  • It belongs to the ha that means "him" or "his" when it stands between two vowelled letters.
  • Smaller silah: no hamzah follows. The damma is read as a waw and the kasra as a ya, for two counts.
  • Greater silah: a hamzah follows. It is held for four or five counts, like madd munfasil.

The four kinds of madd lazim

  • In a word, heavy (kalimi muthaqqal): the madd letter is followed by a shaddah in the same word.
  • In a word, light (kalimi mukhaffaf): the madd letter is followed by a plain sukun in the same word. It occurs in one word in the Quran, in two places in Surah Yunus.
  • In a letter, heavy (harfi muthaqqal): in the separate letters at the start of some surahs, when the letter merges into the next, as with the laam of Alif Laam Meem.
  • In a letter, light (harfi mukhaffaf): in those separate letters when there is no merging, as with Qaaf and Noon.
  • All four are held for six counts.

The separate letters (huroof muqatta'at)

  • The letters of "naqasa asalukum" (noon, qaaf, saad, ayn, seen, laam, kaaf, meem) are held for six counts. Ayn may also be held for four.
  • The letters of "hayyun tahur" (ha, ya, taa, ha, raa) are held for two counts.
  • Alif has no madd.

Examples

  • اٰمَنُوْاMadd badal
  • عَلِیْمًاMadd iwad when stopping
  • اِنَّهٗ كَانَSmaller silah
  • الضَّآلِّیْنَMadd lazim: in a word, heavy
  • الٓمّٓMadd lazim in letters
  • قٓMadd lazim: in a letter, light

Level 3 · AdvancedThe Rules of Laam

The laam in the name of Allah and the laam of "al".

The laam in the name "Allah"

  • If the letter before it has a fatha or a damma, the laam is read heavy.
  • If the letter before it has a kasra, the laam is read light.

The laam of "al"

  • Before the fourteen moon letters, the laam is read clearly. These are called huroof qamariyyah.
  • Before the fourteen sun letters, the laam is not read and the next letter takes a shaddah. These are called huroof shamsiyyah.
  • An easy sign: if the letter after "al" has a shaddah, it is a sun letter.

Examples

  • هُوَ اللّٰهُHeavy laam
  • بِسْمِ اللّٰهِLight laam
  • الْقَمَرِMoon letter: laam is read
  • الشَّمْسِSun letter: laam is not read

Level 3 · AdvancedThe Rules of Raa

When raa is heavy and when it is light.

Raa is heavy

  • When it has a fatha or a damma.
  • When it has a sukun and the letter before it has a fatha or a damma.
  • When it has a sukun after a kasra that is not part of the word, or when a heavy letter follows it in the same word.

Raa is light

  • When it has a kasra.
  • When it has a sukun and the letter before it has a true kasra, with no heavy letter after it in the same word.
  • When we stop on a raa that comes after a ya sakinah.

Take care

  • Raa must not be rolled again and again. The tongue touches only once.

Examples

  • رَبِّHeavy: raa with fatha
  • رُسُلٌHeavy: raa with damma
  • رِجَالٌLight: raa with kasra
  • فِرْعَوْنَLight: sukun after a kasra
  • مِرْصَادًاHeavy: a heavy letter follows

Level 3 · AdvancedIdgham: Merging Two Letters

The three ways one letter merges into the next.

What idgham is

  • Idgham means merging a letter with sukun into the next letter, so that they are read as one letter with a shaddah.
  • A shaddah written on the second letter is the sign.

1. Mithlayn (the same letter)

  • The two letters are the same, such as ba followed by ba or daal followed by daal.
  • The first is always merged into the second.

2. Mutajanisayn (same makhraj)

  • The two letters come from the same makhraj but differ in their qualities.
  • The groups are: taa, daal and taa (ت د ط); thaa, dhaal and zaa (ث ذ ظ); and ba with meem.

3. Mutaqaribayn (close makhraj)

  • The two letters come from makhraj that are close to each other.
  • In the recitation of Hafs this happens with laam into raa, and with qaaf into kaaf.

Complete and incomplete

  • In complete idgham (tamm) nothing of the first letter remains.
  • In incomplete idgham (naqis) a quality of the first letter remains, such as the heaviness of taa (ط) when it merges into taa (ت), or the ghunnah of noon when it merges into ya or waw.

Examples

  • اذْهَبْ بِّكِتٰبِیْMithlayn: ba into ba
  • وَقَدْ دَّخَلُوْاMithlayn: daal into daal
  • قَدْ تَّبَیَّنَMutajanisayn: daal into taa
  • قَالَتْ طَّآىِٕفَةٌMutajanisayn: taa into taa (ط)
  • ارْكَبْ مَّعَنَاMutajanisayn: ba into meem
  • قُلْ رَّبِّMutaqaribayn: laam into raa
  • نَخْلُقْكُّمْMutaqaribayn: qaaf into kaaf

Level 3 · AdvancedStopping and the Signs of Waqf

How to stop correctly and what the signs in the Quran mean.

How to stop

  • When stopping, the last letter is read with a sukun.
  • A double fatha becomes an alif, held for two counts.
  • A round ta (ة) is read as a ha.
  • Do not stop in the middle of a word, and take a breath only where you stop.

The signs in the IndoPak Quran

  • The circle marks the end of an ayah. Stopping there is good.
  • Meem (م): you must stop.
  • Taa (ط): a complete stop. Stopping is better.
  • Jeem (ج): you may stop or continue.
  • Zaa (ز): you may stop, but continuing is better.
  • Saad (ص): stopping is allowed if you need a breath.
  • Laam-alif (لا): do not stop here. If it is at the end of an ayah, you may stop.
  • Seen or the word "saktah": pause briefly without taking a breath.
